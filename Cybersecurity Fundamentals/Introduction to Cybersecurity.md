# 1. What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, systems and digital information from unauthorised access, attacks, damage or theft.

Cybersecurity helps protect sensitive information and ensures that systems remain secure and available to authorised users.

# 2. The CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is a fundamental cybersecurity model consisting of three principles: Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.

## 2.1 Confidentiality

Confidentiality ensures that information is only accessible to authorised individuals.

**Example:** Protecting students' personal information from unauthorised access.

## 2.2 Integrity

Integrity ensures that information remains accurate and is not changed without authorisation.

**Example:** Preventing unauthorised changes to students' examination results.

## 2.3 Availability

Availability ensures that systems and information are accessible to authorised users when needed.

**Example:** Ensuring students can access their university portal when registering for courses.

## 3. Why Do Organisations Need Cybersecurity?

Organisations need cybersecurity to protect sensitive information, prevent financial losses, maintain business operations and build trust with their customers.

### 3.1 Protect Sensitive Information

Cybersecurity helps prevent unauthorised individuals from accessing private information, such as passwords, banking details and medical records.

### 3.2 Prevent Financial Losses

Cyberattacks can result in stolen money, fraud and expensive recovery costs.

### 3.3 Maintain Operations

Cybersecurity helps keep systems and services running so that organisations can continue operating without unnecessary interruptions.

### 3.4 Maintain Trust

Customers and employees need to trust that an organisation will protect their personal information and handle it responsibly.

## 4. Types of Hackers

Hackers can be classified according to their intentions and whether they have permission to access or test a system.

### 4.1 Black-Hat Hackers

Black-hat hackers access systems without permission for malicious purposes, such as stealing information, causing damage or committing fraud.

### 4.2 White-Hat Hackers

White-hat hackers are ethical hackers who have permission to test systems, identify security weaknesses and help organisations improve their security.

### 4.3 Grey-Hat Hackers

Grey-hat hackers may access systems without permission, but they do not necessarily have malicious intentions. Their actions are still unauthorised and may have legal consequences.

### 4.4 Importance of Authorisation

Ethical hacking requires permission from the system owner and clearly defined boundaries.

Good intentions alone do not make unauthorised access ethical.

## 5. Key Takeaways

- Cybersecurity protects computers, networks, systems and digital information.
- The CIA Triad consists of Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability.
- Organisations use cybersecurity to protect information, prevent financial losses and maintain operations.
- Hackers have different intentions and methods.
- Ethical hacking requires permission and clearly defined boundaries.
